Story One — Screen Size

Issue

Bigger screens are tempting — but at what cost?

When shopping for a new television, most buyers focus on picture quality, brand reputation, and above all, screen size. Bigger screens feel more immersive and are often seen as the "better" option, especially for shared living rooms or home theatre setups.

What often gets overlooked is that screen size is not just a viewing preference — it is one of the strongest predictors of how much electricity a television will consume over its lifetime. For buyers who care about their environmental footprint, this is a detail worth understanding before making a purchase.

Demonstrate the Issue

The data shows a clear, steady climb

Looking at labelled energy consumption data across the Australian market, a clear pattern emerges: average yearly energy use rises sharply and consistently as screen size increases, moving from small to medium to large categories.

This isn't a small difference — it's a substantial jump at every step up in size, meaning the size decision alone can significantly shape a household's ongoing electricity use.

Ideas for Overcoming

What can an environmentally-conscious buyer actually do?

Once the link between size and energy use is clear, buyers have several practical ways to reduce their footprint without giving up on a satisfying home viewing experience.

  • Match screen size to actual room size and typical viewing distance, rather than defaulting to the largest option available.
  • Check the energy rating label on the TV, not just the screen size, since ratings can vary between models of the same size.
  • Consider whether a slightly smaller screen would still meet household needs for daily viewing.
  • Factor ongoing running costs into the purchase decision, not just the upfront price of the television.
Describe the Approach

A simple size-matching framework

A practical approach for buyers is to size their TV to their room, not to what looks impressive in a showroom. As a general guide, a medium TV comfortably suits most living rooms and everyday viewing distances, while a large TV is best reserved for genuinely spacious rooms or dedicated home theatre spaces.

Using this simple framework, many households could reasonably step down one size category from what they might otherwise choose, without noticeably affecting their viewing experience.

Recommendation

Choose the smallest screen size that meets your needs

If lowering your household's energy footprint matters to you, prioritise screen size above other features when comparing televisions. A well-matched medium TV can deliver a satisfying viewing experience while using significantly less energy than a large one over its lifetime.

Story Two — Screen Technology

Issue

Not all screens are built the same

Screen size is only one part of the picture. Even two televisions of the exact same size can use noticeably different amounts of energy depending on the underlying display technology — LCD, LCD (LED), or OLED.

Many buyers assume that display technology mainly affects picture quality and price, without realising it can also influence ongoing electricity use, sometimes in ways that aren't obvious from marketing material alone.

Demonstrate the Issue

OLED trends higher at larger sizes

Breaking the data down by both size and screen technology reveals a more detailed pattern. Across medium and large TVs, OLED screens consistently use somewhat more energy than LCD and LCD (LED) alternatives of the same size.

Interestingly, this pattern reverses at small screen sizes, where OLED televisions actually use less energy than their LCD counterparts — a reminder that these effects can shift depending on size, and shouldn't be assumed to apply universally.

Ideas for Overcoming

How buyers can factor technology into their decision

  • Compare the specific energy rating of individual models rather than assuming all screens of a given technology behave identically.
  • Weigh picture quality preferences against the modest energy trade-off, especially for larger screens where the gap is more noticeable.
  • For small TVs, note that OLED may actually be the more efficient choice, not the less efficient one.
  • Ask retailers directly about labelled energy consumption figures when comparing similarly sized models.
Describe the Approach

A two-step decision process

Once a buyer has decided on a screen size using the framework from Story One, screen technology becomes a useful second-level decision. At medium and large sizes, choosing LCD (LED) over OLED offers a genuine, measurable energy saving.

At small sizes, the opposite applies — OLED is the slightly more efficient option — so the "best" choice depends on where the screen size falls, not a single fixed rule.

Recommendation

Let screen size guide you first, technology second

Decide on the right screen size for your space first, since this has by far the largest impact on energy use. Once that's settled, use screen technology as a secondary refinement: favour LCD (LED) at medium and large sizes, and don't discount OLED at small sizes, where it can actually be the more efficient pick.
