Does TV Size Really Matter
for Your Energy Bill?

A closer look at how screen size and screen technology affect energy consumption — for buyers who care about their footprint.

Scroll to explore ↓
01 — The Big Picture

Size Matters Most

On average, moving from a small TV to a large TV nearly quadruples labelled energy consumption — from around 155 kWh per year for small TVs, up to almost 750 kWh per year for large TVs. If reducing your energy footprint is a priority, screen size is the single biggest factor to consider.

02 — Adding Nuance

Screen Technology Also Plays a Role

Size isn't the whole story. Within each size category, screen technology also influences energy use. LCD and LCD (LED) screens consume similar amounts of energy, while OLED screens tend to use slightly more at medium and large sizes — though interestingly, OLED usage drops below the others at small sizes.

03 — Takeaway

What This Means If You're Buying Green

If minimising energy use is your priority, screen size should be your first consideration — a smaller TV will almost always use dramatically less energy than a larger one, regardless of technology. If you also care about display type, choosing an LCD (non-OLED) option at larger sizes offers a small additional efficiency benefit.
